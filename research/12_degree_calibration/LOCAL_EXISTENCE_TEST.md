# Local existence is not global calibration

**2026-10-03 — result B, at the fixed arithmetic-line presentation.** The unrestricted cQ3 local structure maps can be constructed for the native pilot and determinant fibers, using free coefficients. Their existence does not identify the comparison induced by Gamma_chi or imply a native global degree bound. This corrects the emphasis on bare Hom-set nonemptiness in stage 11; it does not establish an IUT counterexample.

We retain stage 11's [Gamma_chi and joint choice record](../11_evaluation_instantiation/PROOF_ATTEMPT.md). Fix its receiving column, an actual bounded source hull H, and its corrected determinant D_H at a common permitted exponent M. The assertions below assume these objects and the local base identifications in this presentation, as cQ3 does. They do not construct H from unspecified algorithm outputs.

## 1. Exact arithmetic control over Q

Give Z_p and [-1,1] reference measure one. At the chosen prime p take Q_p=p Z_p and H_p=p^2 Z_p; at infinity take Q_infinity=H_infinity=[-1,1]; elsewhere both are unit lattices. The exact global volume products are

`mu(Q)=1/p`, `mu(H)=1/p^2`, hence `a=-log(p)`, `b=-2 log(p)`.

The independent coefficients t_p=p and t_v=1 elsewhere give t_v Q_v=H_v at every place. Their total norm is 1/p. Thus all local maps are isomorphisms onto the target regions, but b<a. The volume change is Delta=-log(p) and the containment slack is S=0.

A single global coefficient p instead has absolute values |p|_p=1/p and |p|_infinity=p. Its total norm is exactly one, but its real image is [-p,p], which is not contained in [-1,1]. More generally, no nonzero rational coefficient can satisfy every containment here: the finite conditions force its valuations to be at least 1 at p and nonnegative elsewhere, so it is a nonzero integer divisible by p; its real absolute value is at least p.

The invalid inference is from independently chosen local isomorphic containments to a degree inequality for the **original** global object. A sufficient added hypothesis is that the *specified jointly admissible comparison* has zero total logarithmic norm, or more generally a defect Delta with Delta+S>=0. Delta>=0 together with S>=0 is a useful stronger sufficient condition. A principal global coefficient is one way to obtain Delta=0; it is not necessary. This control is ordinary arithmetic, not a model of the IUT bridge.

## 2. The actual model equation and its sign

[FrI](../../sources/primary/Frobenioids_I.pdf), Theorem 5.2(i), printed/PDF p.100, constructs objects A=(A_D,alpha), B=(B_D,beta). For a fixed base arrow f:A_D->B_D, a morphism has Frobenius degree n, an effective zero divisor z in Phi(A_D), and a coefficient u in B(A_D), satisfying

\[
n\alpha+z=f^*\beta+\operatorname{Div}_B(u).                 \tag{L1}
\]

Linear means n=1; isometric means z=0 (Definition 1.2(i), p.21). Neither word alone specifies an ambient inclusion with coefficient 1. For phi:A->B and psi:B->C, with base arrows f,g and degrees n,m, the source composition convention is

\[
z_{\psi\phi}=f^*z_\psi+m z_\phi,\qquad
u_{\psi\phi}=f^*u_\psi+m u_\phi,\qquad n_{\psi\phi}=mn,
\tag{L2}
\]

writing the coefficient group additively. In multiplicative notation the coefficient is f^*(u_psi)u_phi^m. Substitution in (L1) verifies (L2), including the direction of pullback.

For fractional ideals pi^a O -> pi^b O over the same discrete valuation field, the object's model coordinate is alpha=-a, beta=-b. Multiplication by t with ord_pi(t)=k has

`z=a+k-b`.

Its effectivity is exactly k+a>=b, the condition t pi^a O subseteq pi^b O. For Q_p,H_p the choice k=1 gives z=0, whereas the **fixed** coefficient t=1 gives z=-1 and is not a morphism. This checks the sign against geometry instead of identifying alpha with an ideal's positive valuation.

## 3. Nonarchimedean fibers of the fixed presentation

The general criterion for fixed f and free u is

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm lin,f}(A,B)\ne\varnothing
\iff (f^*\beta-\alpha+\operatorname{im}\operatorname{Div}_B)
       \cap\Phi(A_D)\ne\varnothing .                    \tag{L3}
\]

For a p-adic model as in [FrII](../../sources/primary/Frobenioids_II.pdf), Example 1.1, pp.7-8, the coefficient divisor image is a nonzero subgroup of the ordered rank-one divisor group. A sufficiently positive multiple of one positive element makes f^*beta-alpha+Div_B(u)>=0. Thus a linear morphism exists for each supplied base arrow. This argument does not require the difference itself to be principal; an isomorphism requires the stronger z=0 and an invertible base arrow.

The relevant *split* native prime-strips give more. At a bad place their divisor monoid is generated by the given root q, with its splitting; at a good finite place it is generated by p_v. See [I](../../sources/primary/IUT_I.pdf), Example 3.2(iv), p.71, and Example 3.3(i), p.78. The split x-mu model in [II](../../sources/primary/IUT_II.pdf), Definition 4.9(iii)-(iv), pp.155-156, has coefficient monoid obtained from the splitting monoid times units. Consequently, in this fixed split model the splitting supplies a section of Div_B after groupification. For alpha and f^*beta in its divisor group choose

\[
u=s(\alpha-f^*\beta),\qquad z=0.                         \tag{L4}
\]

This is an actual model morphism. With a base isomorphism its inverse is obtained by pulling back u^(-1) along the inverse base arrow. At bad places s uses the prescribed q=q_Tate^(1/(2 ell)), modulo its stated torsion; it does not introduce a new root. At a common powered presentation the exponents belong to that presentation's group. We do not replace a nondivisible monoid by its perfection or take unauthorized roots. If a proposed determinant has not yet been put in this local category, (L4) does not do that work; our fixed D_H is assumed to be the Ob3 object.

The local maps just constructed can change the native lattice's measured size. Indeed a local isomorphism may carry pi O to pi^2 O. That is consistent with the source warning in III 3.12(xii), pp.185-186.

## 4. Archimedean places and torsion systems

FrII Example 3.3(i)-(ii), pp.27-28, defines linear maps as vector-space isomorphisms carrying one angular region into the other. For the **arithmetic-line** objects in this test, the regions are isotropic balls (or real intervals): write them r_Q O and r_H O in chosen line trivializations. Multiplication by the positive scalar r_H/r_Q is an isomorphism of these regions. It is an auxiliary coefficient, not a canonical native comparison. Its zero divisor is log(r_H/(|t|r_Q))=0, in agreement with the source definition.

For the full x-mu data, retain II Definition 4.9(v), p.157: an inductive system of quotients by N-torsion, with a cofinal multiplicative index set. The same scalar map commutes with roots of unity and hence induces maps on all these quotients, commuting with the transition maps. This supplies a compatible local system; it is not a choice of unrelated complex representatives at each N.

This argument covers the pilot/determinant arithmetic-line fibers. Arbitrary non-isotropic angular regions need not be related by such a scalar: angular inclusion can be an obstruction. No essential-surjectivity claim for the entire cQ3 category, its hull-approximant subcategory, or arbitrary base arrows lacking a lift is made.

## 5. What cQ3 actually couples

III Remark 3.9.5(ix), pp.141-142, and the frozen [stage 7 calculation](../07_formal_lift/CQ3_FUNCTOR_OR_PAIR.md) give upper objects

`((X_v,[a_v:X_v->D_H,v])_v, X_g, (rho_X,v)_v)`.

The rho_X,v identify the realified local **objects** with localizations of X_g. There is no field a_g:X_g->D_H,g in this object record. In particular it does not impose

\[
\operatorname{loc}_v(a_g)\rho_{X,v}
 =\rho_{D_H,v}\,r_v(a_v).                                \tag{L5}
\]

Morphisms *between* upper objects are compatible local/global collections and respect the structure-map orbits. That condition does not add (L5) to each object's existing local structure maps. Taking Aut(D_H,v)-orbits does not add it either.

Apply (L4) and the archimedean construction to the native Q_M tuple, keeping its global object and all rho_Q,v unchanged. At this fixed arithmetic-line presentation they supply an ordinary object of the **unrestricted** upper cQ3 category lying over Q_M. The maps can be identities outside the finite support of the two arithmetic-line records. All relevant places and the archimedean torsion system are included. This is a direct proof of this particular ordinary fiber's nonemptiness, not an inference from the rational diagnostic or an assertion that all fibers of U_H are nonempty.

It does not put Q_M into the optional hull-approximant subcategory or supply the output of Gamma_chi. Nor does it prove a global morphism a_g satisfying (L5). Thus stage 11's equivalence/Hom-set transport observation remains correct, but bare local nonemptiness is not the remaining obstacle for these fibers. Requiring globally compatible *structure maps* would be an additional sufficient implementation, not part of cQ3's object definition.

The additional source construction asserted to govern the selected comparison is III 3.11.1(iii)'s IPL/SHE output, using the evaluation compatibilities already instantiated in stage 11, with the sign and product-formula qualifications on p.161. cQ4 controls the vertical log adjustment. We do not replace those assertions by the arbitrary local coefficients above.

## 6. Global effectivity is a different test

For an actual global linear morphism over the fixed global base in the arithmetic model, (L1) gives

`deg(beta)-deg(alpha)=deg(z)>=0`,

because Example 6.3, FrI pp.112-114, makes deg(Div_B(u))=0. Localization restricts the *same* coefficient and divisors (FrII Example 5.6(iii)-(iv), pp.63-65). In the realification, the coefficient divisor can be a real linear combination of principal divisors; its degree still vanishes. A single global field element is therefore sufficient, not necessary, for this cancellation.

III Example 3.6(ii), pp.107-108, gives this global test directly: an elementary morphism between collections of fractional ideals is one f in F_mod^x integral at **every** place. It does not allow independent f_v. The corresponding degree constraint is already substantive; declaring such a global morphism to exist would not prove it. The actual IUT route may instead use a weaker formal comparison, so no strict global morphism is imposed as necessary.

The distinctions are now explicit: free linear morphism; local isomorphism; ambient comparison with prescribed coefficient; effective comparison for that coefficient; comparison induced by Gamma_chi; and a jointly admissible, degree-calibrated global/formal comparison. Only the first two have been constructed here. The [degree calculation](GAMMA_DEGREE_CALCULATION.md) addresses the remaining distinction.
